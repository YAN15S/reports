What the research said

Top pain points

  • Ad claims read as dramatic ("erase wrinkles") while real results are subtle and gradual; two of this product's own critical reviews say exactly this
  • No immediate sensory feedback (no tingle, no peel), so buyers assume "it's not working" and quit around week 2 to 4, before real change shows
  • Fear of counterfeit "K-beauty" dupes online; one verified reviewer describes returning a fake peptide product that was "basically water with gold flecks"
  • Price volatility feels unfair: the same listing seen at $60 one day and $37 the next erodes trust in the purchase itself
  • Real monthly cost (~$45 to 50 at the labeled dosage) creates budget guilt against an uncertain payoff
  • Aging, sensitized skin reacts poorly to harsher actives like retinol, leaving buyers stuck avoiding the ingredients they're told actually work
  • Skepticism that topical peptides/collagen can even penetrate skin at a molecular level

Top benefits

  • Silky, fast-absorbing, non-sticky-once-dry texture that layers invisibly under the rest of a routine and under makeup
  • Real hydration and next-morning glow, repeatedly confirmed independent of the anti-aging claim
  • Gradual, cumulative firming and softening of fine lines with consistent use (dermatologist-referenced peptide science, not just a marketing word)
  • Skin barrier support beyond cosmetics: one reviewer used it on a sunburn and reported the burn faded noticeably faster
  • Genuine K-beauty formulation halo ("Korean skin care just delivers") that reads as trustworthy versus Western marketing hype
  • Precision dropper applicator gives controlled, low-waste dosing, and reviewers confirm "a little goes a long way"

What competitors run

  • 10 of 16 competitor ads lean on Korean silk protein / "glass skin" / needle-free lift positioning (GlassLab, Korean Skincare Reviews, Saeskyn, Navaro, Emily Williams' personalization quiz)
  • 4 of 16 sell a multi-peptide "science stack" claim (Geek&Gorgeous "6 peptides, 1 formula", Maelove "4 peptide classes", Isomers "4 Matrixyl technologies")
  • 2 of 16 run a long-form "Surgery In A Bottle" advertorial positioning the product against $5,000, $23,000 facelifts/Botox (Jessica Lundgren, Ruby S. Jones, both funneling to the same olavita.co lander)
  • Zero competitor ads address buyer skepticism directly, engage the "does this even work" scientific objection, or touch self-care/consistency as an emotional payoff on its own; those gaps are what this brief's top 3 picks were built to fill
