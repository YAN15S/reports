Pain points (8)

Taste divisiveness on the existing electrolyte gummy format: a verified 3-star review reads, "They feel like they work to hydrate but they do not taste very good."

Why it happens: The format asks the tongue to carry a sweet gummy and a salty mineral blend at once, and few flavors hide that.

How it feels: Let down, since hydration was the whole point of buying it.

How we solve it: A flavor the buyer would actually keep taking is the starting point.

Amazon listing, customer reviews

Price sensitivity even from satisfied buyers: one reviewer wrote, "I love the taste... The cost is a little pricey."

Why it happens: Gummies are priced per bag, and a buyer who feels no clear difference starts counting the cost.

How it feels: Mildly cheated, even when the taste is fine.

How we solve it: A visible per-serving dose on the label so the cost reads as value.

Amazon listing, customer reviews

Dosage-fraud skepticism specific to creatine gummies, voiced in a Slickdeals user comment: "99% of creatine gummies have no creatine in them."

Why it happens: Gummy supplements have a reputation for under-dosing, and buyers cannot taste or see the dose.

How it feels: Suspicious, like being sold candy.

How we solve it: The creatine and electrolyte amounts printed on the label, per gummy.

Amazon listing, customer reviews

Water retention and bloat fear is a common reason people avoid creatine altogether, especially outside the bodybuilder identity.

Why it happens: Creatine is known to pull water into muscle, and people outside the lifter crowd fear looking puffy.

How it feels: Anxious about a side effect before even starting.

How we solve it: Cannot promise no bloat; set honest expectations on the label and page.

Amazon listing, customer reviews
